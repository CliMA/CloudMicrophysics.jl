P3 internals

This page is a reference for the building blocks of the P3 functions on the API page, grouped by what they compute. Their names, arguments, and behavior can change without notice, so models should call the API functions instead.

Mass relation

P3Scheme.P3Particles evaluates these relations once at construction, from the rime mass fraction and the rime density. The graupel density follows from the density of the unrimed part, and the size thresholds of the mass regimes follow from the densities. The aspect ratio of a particle uses the material density of its regime.

CloudMicrophysics.P3Scheme.get_ρ_d — Function
get_ρ_d(mass, F_rim, ρ_rim)

Return the density of the unrimed portion of the particle [kg/m³].

The result is the exact solution as a function of the rime mass fraction F_rim, the mass power law parameters mass, and the rime density ρ_rim.

For the derivation of the numerically stable form used here, see the (P3 scheme documentation).

Arguments

  • mass: CMP.MassPowerLaw parameters
  • F_rim: rime mass fraction
  • ρ_rim: rime density

Returns

  • ρ_d: density of the unrimed portion of the particle [kg/m³]

Examples

julia> import CloudMicrophysics.Parameters as CMP,
              ClimaParams as CP,
              CloudMicrophysics.P3Scheme as P3

julia> FT = Float64;

julia> mass = CMP.MassPowerLaw(CP.create_toml_dict(FT));

julia> F_rim, ρ_rim = FT(0.5), FT(916.7);

julia> ρ_d = P3.get_ρ_d(mass, F_rim, ρ_rim)

Slope solve

P3Scheme.P3Distribution solves for the slope with get_distribution_logλ. The solve matches log(L/N) from logLdivN, built from the mass moments of logmass_gamma_moment and the gamma moments of loggamma_moment, to the ratio of the ice mass and the bounded number. The shape parameter follows from the slope with get_μ, and the intercept from the number with get_logN₀. logN′ice evaluates the log of the number concentration at a diameter.

CloudMicrophysics.P3Scheme.get_distribution_logλ — Function
get_distribution_logλ(particles, ρq_ice, ρn_ice)

Solve for logλ, the log of the slope parameter of the P3 size distribution.

The solve uses the ice mass ρq_ice, the bounded ice number ρn_ice, and the mass relation of the P3Particles particles.

The assumed distribution is of the form

\[N′(D) = N₀ D^μ e^{-λD}\]

where N′(D) is the number concentration at diameter D and μ is the shape parameter. The shape parameter is parameterized, e.g. CMP.SlopePowerLaw or CMP.SlopeConstant.

This algorithm solves for logλ = log(λ) given ρq_ice and ρn_ice by solving the equations:

\[\begin{align*} \log(L) &= \log ∫_0^∞ m(D) N′(D)\ \mathrm{d}D, \\ \log(N) &= \log ∫_0^∞ N′(D)\ \mathrm{d}D, \\ \end{align*}\]

where m(D) is the mass of a particle at diameter D (see ice_mass). The procedure is described in detail in the P3 docs.

The solve uses the primal (as opposed to ForwardDiff.Dual) values of its arguments and returns a primal value, so logλ is constant under differentiation with respect to the state.

Arguments

  • particles: The P3Particles
  • ρq_ice: The ice mass concentration [kg/m³]
  • ρn_ice: The ice number concentration [1/m³], bounded by bounded_ice_number

The search bounds are logλ_bounds. Without ice mass, the result is the upper bound, the smallest particles. With mass but without number, it is the lower bound, the largest particles.

Compute log(scale ⋅ ∫_0^∞ G(D) D^k dD).

CloudMicrophysics.P3Scheme.loggamma_inc_moment — Function
loggamma_inc_moment(D₁, D₂, μ, logλ, [k = 0], [scale = 1])

Compute log(Iᵏ) where Iᵏ is the following integral:

``I^k = ∫_{D₁}^{D₂} G(D) D^k dD``

$G(D) ≡ D^μ e^{-λD}$ is the (unnormalized) gamma kernel, and k is an arbitrary exponent.

If scale is provided, log(scale ⋅ Iᵏ) is returned.

With appropriate scaling, we can compute useful quantities like:

  • the k-th moment of the ice PSD, $M^k = N₀ I^k$
  • combined power law and moment weighted integrals, $∫_{D₁}^{D₂} (aD^b) D^n K(D) dD ≡ a I^(b + n)$

Arguments

  • D₁: The minimum diameter [m]
  • D₂: The maximum diameter [m]
  • μ: The PSD shape parameter [-]
  • logλ: The log of the PSD slope parameter [log(1/m)]
  • k: An arbitrary exponent [-], default is 0
  • scale: The scale factor [-], default is 1

Extended help

Implementation details

We can write ∫_D₁^D₂ G(D) D^k dD, where G(D) = D^μ e^{-λD} as: ∫_D₁^∞ G(D) D^k dD - ∫_D₂^∞ G(D) D^k dD with the transformation x = λD, and z = μ+k+1, each term can be written as: ∫_{Dᵢ}^∞ G(D) D^k dD = ∫_{λDᵢ}^∞ x^z e^{-x} dx / λ^z = Γ(z, λDᵢ) / λ^z where Γ(z, λDᵢ) = q ⋅ Γ(z) and q is the incomplete gamma function ratio given by (_, q) = UT.gamma_inc(z, x). This means that the integral ∫_{Dᵢ}^∞ G(D) D^k dD is computed as: Γ(z) ⋅ q / λ^z The full integral from D₁ to D₂ is then: Γ(z) ⋅ (q_D₁ - q_D₂) / λ^z In log-space, this is: - z log(λ) + logΓ(z) + log(q_D₁ - q_D₂)

Liquid-ice collisions

P3Scheme.bulk_liquid_ice_collision_sources builds the collision kernel, the local rime density, and the maximum freezing rate for the ice particles of the state. For each ice diameter, get_liquid_integrals integrates the kernel over the liquid size distribution, split at the diameter at which a drop falls as fast as the ice particle. ∫liquid_ice_collisions integrates the result over the ice size distribution, split at the wet-growth onset diameters, and partitions the collected mass between freezing and shedding.

Return the monomial coefficients (k₀, k₁, k₂) of the ice-liquid collision cross-section.

\[σ(Dᵢ, Dₗ) = π (rᵢ + Dₗ/2)² = k₀ + k₁ Dₗ + k₂ Dₗ²,\]

CloudMicrophysics.P3Scheme.compute_local_rime_density — Function
compute_local_rime_density(velocity_params, particles, ρₐ, T)

Return a function ρ′_rim(Dᵢ, Dₗ) that computes the local rime density [kg/m³].

The arguments are the ice particle diameter Dᵢ [m] and the liquid particle diameter Dₗ [m].

Arguments

  • velocity_params: velocity parameterization, e.g. CMP.Chen2022VelType
  • particles: P3Particles
  • ρₐ: air density [kg/m³]
  • T: temperature [K]

Returns

A function that computes the local rime density [kg/m³] using the equation:

\[ρ'_{rim} = a + b R_i + c R_i^2\]

where

\[R_i = -\frac{ 10^6 ⋅ D_{liq} ⋅ |v_{liq} - v_{ice}| }{ 2 T_{sfc} }\]

and $T_{sfc} < 0$ is the sub-zero surface temperature [°C], $D_{liq}$ is the liquid particle diameter [m], $v_{liq/ice}$ is the particle terminal velocity [m/s]. With the $10^6$ factor converting $D_{liq}$ from [m] to [μm], the units of $R_i$ are [μm m s⁻¹ °C⁻¹]. The units of $ρ'_{rim}$ are [kg/m³].

We assume for simplicity that $T_{sfc}$ equals $T$, the ambient air temperature. For real graupel, $T_{sfc}$ is slightly higher than $T$ due to latent heat release of freezing liquid particles onto the ice particle. Morrison & Milbrandt (2013) found little sensitivity to "realistic" increases in $T_{sfc}$.

See also LocalRimeDensity.

Extended help

Implementation follows Cober and List (1993), Eq. 16 and 17. See also the P3 fortran code, microphy_p3.f90, Line 3315-3323, which extends the range of the calculation to $R_i ≤ 12$, the upper limit of which then equals the solid bulk ice density, $ρ_ice = 916.7 kg/m^3$. At and above $T_{sfc} = 0$, the local rime density is $ρ_ice$.

Note that Morrison & Milbrandt (2015) [25] only uses this parameterization for collisions with cloud droplets. For rain drops, they use a value near the solid bulk ice density, $ρ^* = 900 kg/m^3$. We do not consider this distinction, and use this parameterization for all liquid particles.

Quadrature

The P3 integrals split the integration range at the regime thresholds and at the kinks of the integrand, and sum a quadrature over each subinterval.

CloudMicrophysics.Quadrature.integrate — Function
integrate(f, a, b, quad)

Approximate the definite integral ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx using the quadrature rule quad.

Mathematical Background

The integral is transformed to the standard interval [-1, 1]:

 y = (2x - (a+b)) / (b-a)    →    x = (b-a)y/2 + (a+b)/2

with Jacobian dx/dy = (b-a)/2, so that

 ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = (b-a)/2 ∫₋₁¹ f(x(y)) dy
             ≈ (b-a)/2 ∑ᵢ f(xᵢ) · inv_weight_fun(quad, yᵢ) · weight(quad, i)

where yᵢ = node(quad, i), xᵢ = (b-a)yᵢ/2 + (a+b)/2, and the inv_weight_fun/weight factors are rule-specific (see ChebyshevGauss and GaussLegendre).

Arguments

  • f: Function to integrate
  • a, b: Integration bounds. Note: if a ≥ b, or a or b is NaN, zero is returned.
  • quad: Quadrature scheme (a QuadratureRule).

Returns

Approximation to the definite integral ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx

Lookup tables

The quantities in the tables and their use are described in Lookup tables. This section describes how the tables are built and read, the conventions that every table follows, and the steps to add a table.

Building the tables

P3Scheme.with_tables builds the tables in five steps:

  1. P3Scheme.P3TableGrid gives the axes of each table group, a tuple of P3Scheme.TableAxis, with the bounds derived from the parameters and the numbers of points of P3Scheme.TABLE_POINTS.
  2. P3Scheme.table_groups gives the point function of each group, which maps the coordinates of one grid point to the values of the group there by quadrature, and the storage options logs and inner of the group.
  3. P3Scheme.tabulate evaluates the point function at every point of the axes of the group, on all threads for an Array, and by a broadcast where the array type places its elements for any other array type.
  4. P3Scheme.UniformTable stores the values in the float type of the parameters.
  5. P3Scheme.P3Tables holds the tables of all groups and a hash of the parameters they were built from, and with_tables returns a copy of the parameters with the tables as their integration.

Each group is stored before the next one is evaluated, so the build holds the values of one group at a time.

Conventions

  • A point function takes a tuple of the coordinates of one point, in the order of the axes of its group, and returns a number or an SVector of components.
  • The values are per unit ice number, and per unit liquid number or mass, so that a table depends only on the shapes of the size distributions.
  • A component that scales as a power of the coordinates is stored as its logarithm, with logs, so that the interpolation is accurate and the value stays positive.
  • With inner = true, the point function evaluates every point of the first axis at once and returns their values as an SVector, so that the points share the expensive part of the integral. The rime density tables evaluate the points of logR in this way, and the fraction tables those of P3Scheme.number_logit.
  • The coordinates are uniform in the logarithm of a quantity that varies over orders of magnitude, logλ, logρₐ, logx̄, logR and logD.
  • The rime coordinate w = 1 - √(1 - F_rim) of P3Scheme.rime_coordinate replaces F_rim, because the particle properties change fastest near F_rim = 1.
  • P3Scheme.ice_table_key takes the primal value of logλ, so that a ForwardDiff.Dual state is differentiated through the other coordinates only, as in the quadrature.
  • P3Scheme.lookup moves a coordinate outside an axis to the nearest end of the axis.
  • The tables are in the float type of the parameters, and Adapt moves them to a device with the parameters.
  • The fingerprint of P3Tables hashes the parameters that the tables depend on, and the constructors of the parameters from a parameter dictionary check it, see P3Scheme.tables_are_current.

Adding a table

A new table group needs

  1. a family of numbers of points in P3Scheme.TABLE_POINTS, and the axes of the group in the constructor of P3TableGrid,
  2. a point function, and its entry in P3Scheme.table_groups with logs and inner,
  3. a method of the process function for P3Scheme.TabulatedP3State that reads the group with P3Scheme.lookup,
  4. the parameters of the new integrals in table_fingerprint, if the existing ones do not cover them,
  5. a test against the quadrature in test/p3_tables_tests.jl, and the errors of the new quantities in test/p3_tables_accuracy.jl.

test/p3_tables_accuracy.jl also measures the errors of a proposed grid, which helps to choose the numbers of points.

Inverse slope table

The tables use the slope $λ$ as their coordinate, rather than the mean particle mass $\bar{x} = L/N$. The shape parameter $μ$ changes steeply in a narrow range of the mean mass, so a table on the mean mass would need many points there. The left panel shows the slope table at the rime density 400 kg/m³: the mean mass decreases monotonically with the slope, so the table has an inverse. The right panel shows the shape parameter against the mean mass, and the range where its slope against $\log \bar{x}$ exceeds half of its largest value, in gray.

include("plots/P3SlopeTable.jl")

P3Scheme.slope_point evaluates P3Scheme.logLdivN on uniform logλ points. P3Scheme.tabulated_distribution_logλ interpolates the table bilinearly in the rime coordinate and the rime density, finds the segment of logλ that holds the target by a bisection with a fixed number of steps, inverts the target linearly on it, and refines the result with one Newton step on the closed form.

Wet-set tables

An ice particle of diameter $D$ collects the liquid mass $u(D) = L_c\, \hat{u}_c(D) + L_r\, \hat{u}_r(D)$ per unit time, with $\hat{u}_l$ the collected mass per unit liquid mass, and it freezes at most

\[m(D) = c_1 D + c_2 D^{3/2} v(D)^{1/2},\]

the maximum freezing rate $∂_t\mathcal{M}_\text{max}$ of Collisions with liquid droplets. The coefficients $c_1$ and $c_2$ hold the temperature, the air properties and the ventilation coefficients, see P3Scheme.max_freeze_rate_scalars. The particles with $u > m$ form the wet set $W$, which freezes $∫_W m N' \mathrm{d}D$ and sheds $∫_W (u - m) N' \mathrm{d}D$, and the other particles freeze all they collect.

P3Scheme.tabulated_wet_set evaluates this partition in four steps:

  1. A scan of 25 points uniform in $\log D$, between the bounds of the collision integrals from the table of P3Scheme.collision_bounds_point, locates the ends of $W$ as the zeros of $\log(u / m)$. $\hat{u}_l$ comes from the per-particle tables of P3Scheme.collected_point, and $D^{3/2} v^{1/2}$ from the table of P3Scheme.capacity_point.
  2. The fraction tables of P3Scheme.fraction_point and P3Scheme.capacity_fraction_point give the fractions of $∫ \hat{u}_l N' \mathrm{d}D$ and of $M_v = ∫ D^{3/2} v^{1/2} N' \mathrm{d}D$ below each end of $W$, on the number logit of the end, see P3Scheme.number_logit. The fraction of the first moment $∫ D N' \mathrm{d}D$ has a closed form in the incomplete gamma function. The frozen mass is the mass collected outside $W$ plus $c_1 ∫_W D N' \mathrm{d}D + c_2 ∫_W D^{3/2} v^{1/2} N' \mathrm{d}D$, and the shed mass the mass collected on $W$ less the frozen mass on $W$. The smaller of the frozen and the shed mass is computed, and the larger is the collected mass less the smaller, which keeps the relative precision of the smaller.
  3. The trapezoid rule on the scan points, with the frozen fraction $\min(m / u, 1)$ at each point, divides the smaller mass between cloud and rain.
  4. The rime volume of each species is its frozen mass over its mean local rime density. The trapezoid rule gives the ratio of the mean inverse rime density of the frozen mass to that of the collected mass, with the local rime density of each point at the mean impact scale of the mass that a particle collects there, and this ratio scales the rime density table.
